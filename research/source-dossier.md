# Ajay Agrawal — Comprehensive Research Dossier

Subject: Ajay K. Agrawal, Geoffrey Taber Chair in Entrepreneurship and Innovation, Professor of Strategic Management, Rotman School of Management, University of Toronto; founder of the Creative Destruction Lab (agrawal.ca). NOT Ajay Agarwal of Bain Capital Ventures, nor Ajay K. Agrawal the mechanical-engineering combustion researcher.

## TL;DR
- Agrawal is a Canadian economist (b. Vancouver) whose academic training is centered on the University of British Columbia (BSc, PhD 1995–2000; MBA source-conflicted between UBC and London Business School); he has been at Rotman since 2003 and is a Member of the Order of Canada (2022), with a Google Scholar citation total around 28,270.
- His influence rests on the "prediction machines" framing of AI economics (three books with Joshua Gans and Avi Goldfarb) and on founding the Creative Destruction Lab, whose graduate companies have generated over CAD $56 billion in equity value.
- Public compensation records (Ontario Sunshine List) show his University of Toronto salary rising from ~$316K (2014) to ~$493K (recent year); he also served on Genpact's board (2019–2025) and co-founded Kindred (sold to Ocado for US$262M in 2020) and Sanctuary AI.

## SECTION 3: TOP 10 PAPERS BY CITATIONS (Google Scholar)
Methodology note: Google Scholar blocks automated access, so exact per-paper GS counts could not be scraped. Figures below are drawn from Google Scholar snippets or Semantic Scholar (S2, which typically undercounts vs. GS) and are labeled accordingly. His overall GS profile total is ~28,270 citations (Section 5). Ordering by GS count is therefore approximate; venues and years are verified against publisher pages.

1. **Prediction Machines: The Simple Economics of Artificial Intelligence** (Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans, Avi Goldfarb), Harvard Business Review Press, 2018. Citation count: likely several thousand on Google Scholar — his single most-cited work (S2's fragmented ~138 record is not authoritative). Contribution: reframes AI advances as a fall in the cost of "prediction," making prediction cheap and raising the value of complements (human judgment, data, and action). This framework became the dominant lens for business/economics discussion of AI.

2. **Some Simple Economics of Crowdfunding** (Agrawal, Christian Catalini, Avi Goldfarb), Innovation Policy and the Economy, vol. 14, 2014 (NBER WP 19133, 2013). Citation count: ~1,069 (Semantic Scholar). Applies transaction-cost, reputation, and market-design economics to explain crowdfunding's rise; sets the research agenda for the field.

3. **Putting Patents in Context: Exploring Knowledge Transfer from MIT** (Agrawal, Rebecca Henderson), Management Science 48(1):44–60, 2002. Citation count: heavily cited (likely 1,000+ on GS); exact figure not found. Shows that patents capture only a small fraction (~6–10%) of the knowledge transferred out of universities; a landmark in the technology-transfer literature.

4. **University-to-industry knowledge transfer: literature review and unanswered questions** (Agrawal), International Journal of Management Reviews 3(4):285–302, 2001. Citation count: widely cited foundational review; exact figure not found. Framed the research agenda for university–industry knowledge transfer.

5. **The Anchor Tenant Hypothesis: Exploring the Role of Large, Local, R&D-Intensive Firms in Regional Innovation Systems** (Agrawal, Iain Cockburn), International Journal of Industrial Organization 21(9):1227–1253, 2003. Citation count: ~633 (Google Scholar snippet). Introduces the influential "anchor tenant" concept for regional innovation clusters.

6. **Restructuring Research: Communication Costs and the Democratization of University Innovation** (Agrawal, Avi Goldfarb), American Economic Review 98(4):1578–1590, 2008. Citation count: several hundred (exact not found). Uses Bitnet adoption to show that lower communication costs enabled mid-tier universities to collaborate with elite institutions.

7. **Recruiting for Ideas: How Firms Exploit the Prior Inventions of New Hires** (Jasjit Singh, Agrawal), Management Science 57(1):129–150, 2011. Citation count: ~446 (Semantic Scholar). Shows firms hire inventors partly to access and build on their prior patented knowledge.

8. **The Economics of Artificial Intelligence: An Agenda** (Agrawal, Gans, Goldfarb, eds.), University of Chicago Press / NBER, 2019. Citation count: heavily cited as a volume (exact not found). Agenda-setting edited volume for AI economics.

9. **Exploring the Impact of Artificial Intelligence: Prediction versus Judgment** (Agrawal, Gans, Goldfarb), Information Economics and Policy 47:1–6, 2019 (NBER WP 24626). Citation count: several hundred (exact not found). Models AI as cheaper prediction and analyzes which human labor is a substitute vs. complement (judgment).

10. **Prediction, Judgment, and Complexity: A Theory of Decision-Making and Artificial Intelligence** (Agrawal, Gans, Goldfarb), chapter in The Economics of AI: An Agenda, 2019 (NBER WP 24243). Citation count: several hundred (exact not found). Formal theory of how AI prediction complements human judgment.

## SECTION 6: NONPROFIT AND INSTITUTIONAL WORK
**Creative Destruction Lab (CDL):**
- Founded 2012 by Ajay Agrawal at the Rotman School of Management, University of Toronto.
- Mission: "to enhance the commercialization of science for the betterment of humankind"; a not-for-profit, objectives-based mentoring program for massively scalable, seed-stage, science- and technology-based companies — takes no equity and charges no fee.
- CEO: Sonia Sennik (CDL-Global). Headquarters: Toronto.
- Scale: hosted 630+ companies during the 2024/25 program year across 20+ specialized streams (AI, Space, Quantum, Climate, Health, Compute, Oceans, Minerals, etc.).
- **Equity value created: over CAD $56 billion**, per CDL's 2024/25 Graduate Companies page: "Since its inception, CDL alumni have collectively generated over CAD $56 billion in equity value" (630+ companies, 13 global locations). Earlier figures were CAD $28B (2023) and CAD $51B (interim). The original benchmark, per CDL's About page, was "a goal of generating $50 million in equity value created by the graduates of the program within five years."
- Locations: ~13 sites (2024/25) across Canada, the US, Europe, and Australia — Toronto, Vancouver (UBC Sauder), Calgary (Haskayne), Montreal (HEC Montréal), Halifax (Rowe), Oxford (Saïd), Paris (HEC Paris), Atlanta (Georgia Tech Scheller), Madison (Wisconsin), Seattle (UW Foster), Berlin (ESMT), Tartu (Estonia), Melbourne (Monash), with newer Milan (Bocconi) and Texas (Texas A&M) sites; IESE (Spain) also cited. The exact site list varies by year and source.
- Notable mentor: astronaut Chris Hadfield (Space stream).

**NEXT Canada:**
- Co-founded by Agrawal in 2010 as The Next 36; now NEXT Canada, delivering three programs — The Next 36, Next AI, and Next Founders — supporting young entrepreneurs.

**NBER:**
- Research Associate, Productivity, Innovation, and Entrepreneurship program (profile at nber.org/people/ajay_agrawal). Co-chairs the NBER Economics of AI Conference. Associated grant: "Collaborative Research: The Impact of Research Costs on the Direction of Scientific Discovery."
